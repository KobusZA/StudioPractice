Part 1

The choice survives the next placement

Leaving a tool, or placing one door, no longer throws away the type and the line you had just set.

Doors

Place the same door again

After a door goes on the plan, that type stays ready. You can place several in a row. Choosing another type, or leaving the door tool, stops the repeat.

The library also keeps a Recently used row at the bottom, for doors placed in this session. It sits under the full library and does not replace it. Opening another job clears that row.

Walls

Wall thickness is remembered

Choosing 220 mm, leaving the wall tool, and starting Walls again still draws 220 mm. The same is true of any other wall type you picked. A deliberate choice of 110 mm still draws 110 mm.

The list no longer jumps back to the first wall in the catalogue every time the tool is started.

Walls

The location line stays selected

Exterior or interior, once chosen, is still the active line after you leave the tool and start Walls again. Changing from 110 mm to 220 mm, or the other way, does not clear that choice.

The control on screen matches the line the next wall will use.

Finishes

Paint shows its bill item

Selecting paint shows which bill item it belongs to. That same item is identifiable on the drawing and in the compiled bill.

If no product is chosen, the row stays labelled as an allowance: “PC allowance, product not chosen.” If the rate book has no rate for that paint, the gap is shown. A rate is not filled in for you.

Walls

New walls red, existing walls white

A wall placed as new work is red on the plan. A wall marked existing is white, and a dark edge keeps it readable on the paper.

Doors, windows, and the other families keep their own colours. This pair is for walls only.

Sheets

PDF openings stay visible

Selecting a wall does not cover the garage doors, doors, and windows on the underlay. You can still see the opening you are trying to match.

One control switches the view: Model puts the drawn walls on top, Sheet puts the PDF on top, openings included. The opacity control is still there.

Finishes

A guideline down from the top

On the wall elevation you can type a distance in millimetres, measured down from the top of the wall. A guideline appears at that height.

A finish boundary can be drawn against that line. Finish quantities and the bill mapping are unchanged.

Part 2

What you measure is what was drawn

The 20 mm that made a “220” wall read wide is named as plaster. Direction is stored before length. Two levels can share one corner.

Walls

A 220 mm wall that measured about 240 mm

The plastered masonry wall stores two thicknesses. The masonry core is 220 mm. The wall on the plan, face to face, is 240 mm. The 20 mm between them is plaster, and the type now reads 220 mm masonry + plaster (240 mm overall). The 110 mm plastered wall, and the plastered pool wall, are named the same way.

  • The stroke, the location line, a face-to-face measure, and the masonry quantity use the overall width.
  • Plaster stays its own coat on the bill. Paint does not thicken the wall.
  • A foundation that is not plastered is labelled with its overall thickness only. It is not described as plaster.

Walls

The wall sits on the line you aimed at

While a wall is being drawn, the direction is chosen before the length. A run close to horizontal or vertical is stored on that axis. A run close to an existing wall, starting on that wall, is stored on that wall’s direction. Any other aim keeps the angle you drew; only the length snaps to a round millimetre.

Typing a length or a heading no longer shifts the ends separately, which was tilting a wall whose start sat off the grid.

Floors

Two floor levels line up

You can pick the same reference point on each level, such as a building corner. The sheets, and the walls drawn from them, share that point. Two levels scaled to the same known dimension land on the same model size.

Alignment moves position. It does not change a wall’s length. Scaling is the only place size changes, and only because you asked for a scale.

Part 3

Edit what is already on the plan

All of this is in the product. A wall can change face, snap, or split. One edge of a floor can move. One Move command covers a sheet, a wall, or a tiled area. A plug stays on its wall, and a floor point stays inside its floor. A floor or a roof can follow the external walls.

Walls

Correct the face of a wall already drawn

Select a wall and switch it to centreline, inner face, or outer face. The wall shifts so the chosen line stays where the old line was, and the joins at both ends stay closed. Centreline is included, including for a wall drawn on a grid.

The same centre, exterior, and interior controls used while drawing appear when that wall is selected.

Walls

Snap and split a selected wall

With exactly one wall selected, Snap and Split appear beside the run. Snap uses the same magnets as drawing a wall: click an end, then the point it should meet. Split takes a click on the wall.

The wall keeps its id. An opening stays on the piece that holds its centre. A finish stays on the piece it covered; a finish that crosses the cut is divided, and a whole-face finish covers both pieces. Modify → Cut still splits at the midpoint.

Floors

Move one floor edge

Select the floor, not a finish on it. Line selects one boundary edge. Dragging that edge moves it, and the other corners stay where they are. The two edges that share the moved line change length so the outline stays closed.

Corner handles still drag. Corner adds a point on an edge. A tile or screed edge is a different edit: it stays inside the floor, and it does not move the outline.

  • One Move command

    Modify → Move repositions one selected wall, the selected sheet, or the tiled area being edited. Click the point to move from, then where that point should land. The landing snaps the same way a new wall does, or a typed distance sets the length. Enter places it. Esc cancels. Undo puts it back. A tiled area stays on its floor. A pinned sheet stays put until it is unpinned.

Electrical

A plug stays on its wall or floor

A single plug, a double plug, or a shaver point stores the wall and the face it was clicked on. It moves when that wall is moved, split, or realigned, and dragging it slides it along the wall. A click that misses the wall places nothing.

A floor light, a recessed floor light, and an underfloor heating point store the floor. They move with that floor, and an edge edit that would leave them outside pulls them back in. A click outside the floor places nothing.

Ceiling plugs, slab plugs, switches, and the other fittings stay free points. A generated cable route to a board is a separate decision, listed with the items that are waiting.

Floors

A floor that follows the walls

While placing a floor or a roof, three choices sit on the plan. Sketch clicks each corner and does not follow the walls until you ask. Room copies the room you click, and follows that room when the room itself follows the walls. External walls takes a click inside the building: the outline follows those walls, so moving a linked wall updates the floor. A roof, and a slab on an upper level, use the same three choices.

On a floor or a roof already placed, Follow external walls ties the outline to the walls that close around it. Stop following walls drops the link. Dragging a corner drops it too, and Line and Corner then edit the boundary by hand.

Already in the product

Two items from the memo needed no build

Undo

Undo is on the toolbar. If a named action cannot be reversed, that is a separate fix.

Redraw, then price

The drawing is the source of the quantities, and the rate book prices them. Finalising the job and redrawing it is the path the app already takes.

Part 4

Still outstanding

These are the requests from the same memorandum that are not in the product yet. Pictures are the ones supplied with the memorandum.

Model and the client document

  • Column size typed and shown live

    A round column and a square column can be placed. Width, depth, or diameter is typed in a field and the plan updates immediately. Selecting a placed column shows those dimensions and accepts a change. How a column is measured on the bill waits on the tube list, below.

  • The client costing names each finish

    Each finish is listed by place, with its extent and its specification: the height of a band, the product or the allowance, and the area. Two finishes on one wall are two costs. The example in the memorandum is 750 mm of mosaic above the kitchen cupboards, and porcelain on the remaining scullery walls.

  • Internal elevations in the client document

    The client document can include an internal elevation of a tiled wall. The elevation shows where each finish starts and stops, and it names the wall it comes from.

  • Installation separate from tile supply

    The client document shows an installation total — measured area, labour, adhesive, grout, spacers, and the other installation materials, with that scope written out — and, separately, a supply allowance per tile type. Each allowance names the area or finish it covers. A low supply allowance cannot sit inside the installation total.

  • Distribution boards and circuits

    More than one distribution board can be placed, including boards on different floors. A circuit belongs to one breaker on one board. Lights and other points can be associated with a circuit. The first electrical point can prompt for a board, and a board placed later can be linked. Load limits are not part of this step, and cable routes are not generated.

Waiting on a decision or a file

Services

Sewer line colours

Sewer lines will use the colour convention you send. Soil, waste, and vent are separate colours only if that list says so.

  • A wall type you can duplicate and thicken

    Duplicate a 220 mm wall as the start of a 280 mm wall, keep it available, and identify it on the bill. This waits until wall types authored in the app are accepted. Types currently come from the firm’s catalogue.

  • Which walls share a bill line

    Walls that need different descriptions or rates must not be grouped together. The grouping rule is still to be confirmed.

  • How columns are measured

    Columns are not measured like walls. The bill needs shape, material, outer size, and steel wall thickness. This waits on the standard square-tube list, and on columns whose size can be typed. I-sections may be a column or a beam, and the drawing and the bill must say which.

  • Tile quantities that roll up and stay traceable

    Wall tile and floor tile are already measured separately, and a wall finish can belong to a room. How tile types and finishes are grouped on the summary bill is still to be agreed. Once that rule exists, a summary quantity still traces back to the room and the surface it came from.

  • The sales wording for the two tile prices

    After installation and supply are separated, the client document says, in plain language, that they are separate so tenders can be compared, and that the supply allowance changes when the client chooses the tiles.

  • An automatic cable route to a board

    This waits on a decision to generate routes. Services are placed symbols and provisional sums. A run can already be drawn by hand. Where there is more than one board, the user confirms which board serves the point.

  • An architect’s bill as the structure

    Keep the architect’s item structure and link measured work to those items. This waits on the bill itself, and on agreement that those item codes become the bill structure. Today the bill is compiled from the model and the firm rate book.

  • The Excel bill as the structure

    The spreadsheet is the reference for how the bill is organised and how quantities are summarised. This waits until that sheet arrives. The columns will not be invented in the meantime.

  • Measuring inside BOS Admin

    This waits until it is clear whether “BOS Admin” means this drawing app or a second product. A sheet can already be scaled here, and quantities already compile from the model.
