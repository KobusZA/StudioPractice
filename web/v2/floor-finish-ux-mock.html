What this proposes

  1. A picture of the floor, first.Selecting a room shows this floor above the fold. Level, wall height and the outline sit behind one collapsed row.
  2. The picture opens the same strip as a wall.The plan shrinks to a marker of which floor. Drag the bar to give the plan more room. Drawing happens on the strip.
  3. The inspector becomes a list.One line per finish, each with its area. Add a finish sits on the strip, beside the copper box, as it does for a wall.
  4. A shared patch is counted, as on a wall.Screed under the tile is “2 finishes”. Where the vinyl also sits on that tile, it is “3 finishes”. Click that patch again to edit the finish underneath.
  5. Nothing is stored until it is placed.Cover whole floor, or Draw area. Screed is the base. Tile, timber, vinyl, carpet and waterproofing go on top. There is no up-the-wall pair: a floor finish is an area.
  6. Line and Corner stay on the view.The selected finish shows its corners and the length of an edge. A finish that follows the whole outline does not offer them until its corners are edited.
  7. Floor finishes opens this view.Choosing a structural floor type stays on Floors. The two commands are not merged.
  8. Wall rules stay off this floor.Finish one wall by selecting it. “Every wall of this room” is not part of the floor view.
